Method and evidence

A shared mathematical framework for structure, historical analogues and validation.

1. Question and scope

How do relationships between assets change, which earlier periods have similar topology, and does that comparison add useful information beyond labelled correlation? Test combines live geometry and historical retrieval on one dataset.

The workspace describes completed observations. Validation estimates later returns at historical held-out origins. It uses a fixed present-day asset basket, so it does not establish a tradable strategy or eliminate survivorship bias.

2. Data and window convention

Use 3–12 distinct labelled assets with consistently adjusted OHLCV and matching dates. The selected window has L completed prices and L−1 log returns, with 10 ≤ L ≤ 120. Rolling scales use 20, 60 and 120 prices, hence 19, 59 and 119 returns. This price-count convention is used throughout Test.

The public Stock adapter supplies the same real history to Test. Test has no provider credential. CSV is validated locally; synthetic data appears only after explicitly choosing the demo and keeps its source label. Missing basket dates disable affected windows. No pairwise deletion or silent asset removal is used.

Every rolling observation ends at or before the selected close. Historical replay uses the data loaded today, including any vendor revisions; completed-session checks alone do not establish point-in-time availability. Unadjusted splits can affect all features.

3. Robust linear and rank geometry

For each asset, winsorize returns at four robust scales around their median. The scale floor is 10⁻⁶. Center and normalize the bounded series. Separately rank the raw returns using average ranks for ties, then center and normalize. Constant or numerically degenerate series make topology unavailable.

Both correlations are Gram matrices of normalized complete columns. Their chord distances are Euclidean distances between unit vectors, so they satisfy the triangle inequality. Distinct identical vectors have zero distance: the labelled construction is a pseudometric. No identity shrinkage is applied to the homology geometry.

4. Vietoris–Rips persistence

At threshold ε, a simplex enters when every pairwise distance among its vertices is at most ε. Test constructs vertices, edges and triangles; triangles fill cycles. These simplices suffice for H₀ and H₁. Boundary reduction is over F₂, ordered by filtration value, then dimension, then lexicographic vertex order.

Only positive-lifetime finite classes are stored. The one essential H₀ class is omitted from diagram matching. With three vertices, a Rips triangle fills at the last edge, so a positive-lifetime H₁ class cannot occur. The drawn network is a circular labelled layout, not a distance embedding.

5. Exact diagram transport and retrieval distance

Test consistently uses Wasserstein order two with an L∞ ground norm. Unmatched points are paired with the diagonal at cost ((d−b)/2)². Exact assignment includes explicit diagonal copies. This differs from the Euclidean-ground distance used in the standalone Homology v0.5 workspace.

Each channel and dimension distance is divided by √p. The two channels have fixed weights 0.7 and 0.3; H₀ and H₁ have equal weights. These are declared engineering settings, not learned probabilities. Diagram matching discards vertex identity, even when the same labelled basket is required.

6. Profiles and rolling deformation

Energy, entropy, longest lifetime, barcodes and the first landscape layer summarize a window. Lifetimes refer to filtration thresholds, not the number of calendar days. W₂ change compares consecutive diagrams at the same scale and channel. Its first value, and changes across an unavailable window, are left missing. Acceleration is a discrete difference in those changes.

The rolling timeline stops at the selected close. Empty H₁ diagrams can produce zero change despite changes in H₀ or labelled relationships. Cross-scale plots share observations and are not independent evidence.

7. Past-only historical analogues

Candidate windows have the same length and basket as the query and are sampled every five primary-symbol sessions. For each h in {5,20,60}, the entire candidate outcome must end strictly before the query starts. Each horizon has a separate mature cohort. Candidates are sorted by structural distance; greedy references are separated by at least L+h sessions.

Later returns refer to the selected stock, while geometry refers to the basket. Display the empirical distribution and opposite-sign outcomes. Structural distance is a dissimilarity, without a probability or percentage-similarity interpretation.

8. Matching stability and abstention

Eight fixed-seed joint circular-block resamples perturb the query return vectors, preserving simultaneous cross-asset indices. The block length is max(2,round(√(L−1))). Each horizon compares against a local fixed shortlist of at most 30 candidates, with the original first three references retained if spacing moved them outside that shortlist.

Top-reference and reference-set retention measure local sensitivity. They are not confidence intervals. Short queries, too few references, tied distances, excessive clipping, insufficient valid resamples or retention below 0.5 limit evidence. Validation scores only origins marked observed.

9. Validation and ablation

Validation uses 30-price queries and a 20-session outcome, moving origins every 20 sessions. Retrieve at most six separated references and use their mean later return as the historical estimate. The past-only baseline is the mean of mature spaced past 20-session returns available before the query.

Run full H₀+H₁, H₀-only, H₁-only and labelled correlation-distance retrieval with identical availability, spacing and stability rules. For labelled correlation, compare the upper triangles of the chord-distance matrices using weighted RMS distance. H₀/H₁ ablations use the corresponding channel-weighted diagram distance. Report each method’s coverage; compare MAE only on dates scored by every method.

Report attempted, scored and abstained origins, MAE and paired mean error differences. Fewer than ten scored origins cannot support a reliable forecasting conclusion; ten is a display threshold, not a statistical guarantee. Common-origin results are conditional on passing every method’s gate. A predeclared independent holdout, paired uncertainty estimates, alternate universes and transaction-cost evaluation remain further research.

10. Supporting observations

Asset relationships retain labels and apply identity shrinkage for descriptive estimation; this differs intentionally from the unshrunk topology geometry. Basket risk uses equal weights and reports variance contributions. Multiscale state, price paths, return distributions, candles and price–volume order provide context. They do not form a voting score.

11. Reproducibility and review

Export the exact query, source, adjustment convention, asset labels, selected diagrams, rolling profiles, historical cohorts and validation records. Worker calculations are cancelled when the selection changes. Numerical tests cover exact complexes, diagram assignment, asset permutations, empty diagrams, prefix isolation and shared-window equality.

The earlier 30-stock fitted Forecast and Backtest require an unpublished verified replay snapshot. Test retains useful visual forms with fresh calculations from the current basket; those older fitted outputs are not displayed as current results.
